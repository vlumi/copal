# What the survey makes of Gondwana

Part 2 of [third-specimen.md](third-specimen.md): the survey run on Gondwana Life Insurance as it was after part 1, unchanged, and every place it breaks or comes out wrong. Each is a coupling to the first two specimens, to ASCII, to cents or to the Western calendar, to be fixed generically in the part named, never by special-casing Gondwana.

## How it was found

As is, the survey reads none of Gondwana's 14 programs, and stops before any of the batch files. To see past the first errors, a throwaway copy of the analyzer was patched just enough to go on, and so was a copy of two programs for the one construct cobolith cannot get past, the Report Writer, which was taken out of them. With those, all 14 programs parse, the seven batch files read as jobs, and the blueprints are written; what they say was then read against the code. None of the patches is kept. There are no golden blueprints of Gondwana yet; the first come with part 3.

One thing did not break that was expected to: the lexer. Read as ASCII, the source lexes into the same tokens as read as Shift_JIS, because the names are half-width katakana, one byte each, and the kanji are all in comments and literals, which the lexer takes whole. The ASCII reading gets by on reading; it is on writing that it fails, as finding 4 says.

## Where the survey stops

Each of these stops the survey of every program.

- **1. A copybook of procedure.** Every file in the copybook directory is read as data description entries, so `PRDLOD` and `RATLOD`, the loading of the tables as paragraphs to `COPY` into the procedure division, stop with "expected a number, found ｼｮｳﾋﾝ-ﾖﾐｺﾐ-RTN", and with them the survey of every program, those that copy them and those that do not. A copybook is whatever is copied; one whose entries are paragraphs is a copybook of procedure, read where it is copied and not as a record. Part 3.
- **2. The Report Writer.** cobolith stops at `FD REPORT-F REPORT IS RESERVE-RPT` with "REPORT is not supported yet": GLM020 and GLM030. Behind it are the `REPORT SECTION`, the `RD` with its controls and page limits, the report groups with `LINE`, `COLUMN`, `SOURCE`, `SUM` and `VALUE`, `INITIATE`, `GENERATE` and `TERMINATE`, and `PAGE-COUNTER`. A report group is what the report file is written from, and its `SOURCE` and `SUM` items are the origins of its columns. Part 3, a cobolith release first.
- **3. Batch files taken for programs.** The survey tells a job by its extension, `.jcl` or `.sh`; `ANNUAL.BAT` is lexed as COBOL and stops at "unknown indicator 'o'", the `o` of `@echo off`. Bedrock reads batch files into the same model as shell scripts since #138; the survey has not been told. Part 4.

## Where it comes out wrong

With the throwaway patches, these facts are wrong or missing.

### The encoding, part 3

- **4. The blueprints are not UTF-8.** Every name, comment and literal is written as the Shift_JIS bytes it was read as: 72 of the 125 files are not valid UTF-8, and a Markdown viewer or a browser shows them as mojibake. The JSON files are worse: Go's encoder puts U+FFFD in place of every byte it cannot take, 12,521 times in `entities.json` and 7,933 in `programs/GLD010.json`, so the names in them are gone, not garbled. The anchors in each page's contents are made of the bytes that survived the slug: `#ﾒｳ-rtn` for ﾒｲﾝ-RTN, and `#-rtn`, `#-rtn-1` and `#-rtn-2` for three paragraphs of GLD060, so a link goes to the wrong paragraph or nowhere. The explorer would serve the same bytes, and the survey's own error messages carry them. The source must be read in the dialect's encoding, `ReadProgramIn`, `ReadIn` and an encoding-aware `ReadDataEntries`, and what the survey quotes of it transcoded to UTF-8 once, at the boundary, with the anchors made from the transcoded names.

### Kinds, part 4

- **5. Yen are numbers and rates are amounts.** Of the 17 copybooks' fields, eight are amounts and 86 numbers, and the eight are the ones with decimals: the premium rate per 1,000 yen, the cash-value and reserve factors, the hospital rate and the dividend rate. Every amount in yen is a number: the sum insured and the premium in POLMST, the debit in ZENGIN and the totals of its trailer, the journal's and the payment's amounts. An amount is told by its decimals, and a currency without them turns the kinds inside out. What else tells an amount: it is summed into a total, multiplied by a rate, compared with another amount, written to a trailer's total, and named for one in the system's own language, ｷﾝｶﾞｸ.
- **6. No field is a date.** The date kind is told by the name, `DATE` in it; Gondwana's names end in ﾋﾞ, ﾋﾂﾞｹ, ﾈﾝｹﾞﾂ and ﾈﾝ. So PM-ｹｲﾔｸﾋﾞ, the contract date, is text, CT-ｷﾞｮｳﾑﾋﾂﾞｹ, the business date, a number, PM-ﾊﾗｲｺﾐｽﾞﾐﾈﾝｹﾞﾂ, the paid-to month, a number, and the dates of the system, in three forms and two calendars, are not gathered as Pangaea's one business date is. Beyond the name, a date is what is passed to GLC020 as LK-ｾｲﾚｷ or LK-ﾜﾚｷ, an argument of `INTEGER-OF-DATE`, a field compared with the business date, and a field whose digits are taken apart as a year and a month, by a redefinition into WS-YYYY and WS-MM or by `(1:6)` and `(4:4)`.
- **7. An era date is cut from its Western one.** PM-ｹｲﾔｸﾋﾞ's origin in GLD010 is "call GLC020 argument 3 WS-ｹｲﾔｸﾋﾞ-ﾜﾚｷ" and stops there; the Western date it came from, NP-ｹｲﾔｸﾋﾞ in the sales system's file, is argument 2 of the same call, and inside GLC020 the one is computed from the other and the era table's literals. Every date in the master passes through that call, so every trail of a date ends at it. The conversion is the contract between the two calendars, the thing a rewrite most needs to see, and lineage through a called program should carry it: the caller's argument 3 comes from the caller's argument 2, through LK-ｾｲﾚｷ and LK-ﾜﾚｷ.
- **8. A year with no era.** AG-ﾆｭｳｼｬﾈﾝｹﾞﾂ, the month an agent joined, is `9(4)`, `YYMM`, and prints as `62/04`, `03/10` and `15/04`: a number to the survey, and to the code, which only prints it. It is a date whose century is nowhere in the system, and should be listed as one, with that said.

### Records and lineage, part 4

- **9. A write performed from the paragraph that set the type.** GLM010 writes four record types to the banks' file: ﾍｯﾀﾞ-RTN sets `ZG-ﾍｯﾀﾞ` and fills the header, then performs PRMREQ-ｶｷｺﾐ-RTN, which writes; ﾃﾞｰﾀ-RTN, ﾄﾚｰﾗ-RTN and the end do the same. The one `WRITE` is given every type, and each type's fields the origins of every type's moves over the same bytes: ZG-H-ｼｭﾍﾞﾂｺｰﾄﾞ, the header's kind, comes "from PM-ｷﾞﾝｺｳｺｰﾄﾞ 188-189, SR-ｷﾞﾝｺｳｺｰﾄﾞ 1-2, literal 0, literal 1, literal 91", where the code moves 91 and nothing else. Pangaea and Laurasia set the type and write in one paragraph. The type a write takes is what the path knows at the `PERFORM` that reaches it.
- **10. A dataset read whole has no copybook.** `$DATA/IN/EXTRACT.TXT`, the extract the system opens from, is read by GLO010 into a 300-byte record and moved whole into PM-ｹｲﾔｸ-ﾚｺｰﾄﾞ; the blueprints give it no copybook, while `$DATA/OUT/POLMST.$DATE.TXT`, which GLD090 writes `FROM` the same record, gets POLMST. Laurasia's `inbound/accounts.txt` is the same, which Pangaea hid. The oracle already lays out a record read whole as the record it is moved into; the datasets should say the same.
- **11. The copybook's banner as a paragraph's description.** ｼｮｳﾋﾝ-ﾖﾐｺﾐ-RTN's description is the whole header of `PRDLOD` and then its own: the comment before the first paragraph of a copybook of procedure is the copybook's, and the paragraph's is what follows it. With finding 1.

### Jobs, part 4

- **12. A sort work file needs no DD.** "Step GLM010 exports no DD_SORTWK for GLM010's file SORT-F", and the same for GLM020 and GLM030 in CLOSE: three findings where there is nothing to find. The JCL binding leaves `SD` files out, `job.go:333`; the script binding does not. Pangaea's TXSORT has the same `SELECT SORT-WORK ASSIGN TO SORTWK` and no finding.
- **13. The batch files, once read.** Through bedrock's model they come out right: DAILY's six steps, the `call` of UNLOAD as a script step, the DD bindings, the stop at errorlevel 8 as "RC >= 8", the business date as `$DATE`, the description from the `rem` lines at the head, and the datasets under `$DATA`. Nothing to fix beyond finding 3; the schedule has only OPEN before the rest, as the files are named by date and not by generation, which is as Laurasia's.

### Rules and states, part 4

- **14. The policy's life is drawn from any.** PM-ｼﾞｮｳﾀｲ's state machine has every transition from `any`: the status is tested in the paragraph that performs the one that sets it. GLD060's ｼｮﾘ-RTN tests ﾕｳﾖﾁｭｳ and ｼｯｺｳ-RTN sets ｼｯｺｳ; GLD020's ｼｮﾘ-RTN tests ﾎｼｮｳﾁｭｳ and ﾏﾝｷ-RTN sets ﾏﾝｷ; GLD030's ｹﾝｻ-RTN tests and ｼﾎﾞｳ-RTN and ｶｲﾔｸ-RTN set; GLD040's ﾌｯｶﾂ-RTN tests ｼｯｺｳ and ﾌｯｶﾂ-ﾊﾝｴｲ-RTN sets ﾕｳｺｳ. Only GLD050 tests and sets in one paragraph, grace to in force and in force to grace, and those two are the whole machine the survey draws: in force, grace, lapse, reinstatement, surrender, maturity and death are the system, and they are missing. A state the path knows at the `PERFORM` should carry into the paragraph performed, as a condition's outcome already does when every path to every `PERFORM` of it knows the same.
- **15. A transition's reasons are the wrong conditions, and it is listed once a rule.** GLD030's ｶｲﾔｸ-RTN sets ｶｲﾔｸ on every one of its paths, and the machine lists the transition 24 times, each with that path's outcomes of the product search and the entry-age bands, which decide the cash value and not the surrender. The reason for a transition is what the path tests of the state and the request, and a transition that every path of the paragraph takes has no reason within it.
- **16. Rules no run can take, two levels up.** GLC020's ﾊﾝｲ-ｹﾝｻ-RTN has 9 rules, two of which find LK-ｹｯｶ not converted on a path that set nothing: ﾒｲﾝ-RTN sets it converted, performs ｾｲﾚｷ-ﾍﾝｶﾝ-RTN, which performs ﾊﾝｲ-ｹﾝｻ-RTN, and nothing in between changes it. A path knows what it was given one `PERFORM` up; here it is two.

## What came out right

Worth saying, since the specimen was written to break things: the layouts, the Fujitsu dialect's `COMP-5 SYNC` fields at the offsets GnuCOBOL gives them, the tables of `VALUE` lines redefined as `OCCURS`, the condition names with two values, the kanji in literals and the era table; the calls by upper-case name with their linkage, in both directions; the sort's input and output procedures as performed; lineage through the sort record, through the table GLD050 buffers the results in, through `INTEGER-OF-DATE`'s arguments, and from the command line as a stream; `OPEN EXTEND` as append and `SELECT OPTIONAL`; the headers and histories of programs, copybooks and batch files as their descriptions; the links between records by key and by equality; and the rules, which read as the code does, the chains of `IF WS-ﾘﾕｳ-ﾅｼ` folded by what the path knows.
