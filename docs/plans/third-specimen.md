# Plan: a third specimen

The survey holds on two systems: a bank in IBM COBOL run by JCL, and a card processor in Micro Focus COBOL run by shell scripts. Both are written in ASCII, in cents, by shops that date their records in the Western calendar, and neither prints a report with the Report Writer. The third big home of COBOL, after banking and government, is insurance, and the biggest COBOL world the survey has not seen is Japan's: Fujitsu, Hitachi and NEC compilers, source and data in Shift_JIS. Gondwana Life Insurance is a life insurer on Fujitsu NetCOBOL, run from Windows batch files, in yen, with dates in the Japanese eras. Pangaea split into Laurasia and Gondwana; the family is complete.

As with Laurasia, the deliverable is not the specimen but what it shows: every place the survey, the oracle or bedrock breaks on it or comes out wrong is a coupling to the first two, fixed generically, never by special-casing Gondwana. Read [second-specimen.md](second-specimen.md) first; its ground rules apply, and this plan says only what is new.

## What it varies, on purpose

| The first two | Gondwana |
| --- | --- |
| ASCII source and data | Shift_JIS source and data, `cobol.Fujitsu`: Japanese in comments, literals, messages and data, and the characters whose second byte is a backslash or a quote among them |
| English names and comments throughout | Japanese throughout: comments, literals, messages and reports in kanji and kana, and data and paragraph names in half-width katakana with an ASCII record prefix, as the code of that world was written; file names stay ASCII, which GnuCOBOL turns into C identifiers |
| Amounts in cents, `V99` | Amounts in whole yen, `PIC 9(9)` with no decimals; rates and factors in `V9(5)`; a kind the survey must tell from a count |
| Dates `YYYYMMDD` | Era dates: an era letter and two digits of year, `S60`, `H07`, `R08`, with Showa, Heisei and Reiwa all in force in one master; Western dates in the files exchanged with the banks; and one table dated by a two-digit year with no era, from the 1980s |
| Overpunched signs | No overpunched signs: unsigned amounts, and `SIGN LEADING SEPARATE` where a sign is needed, as the interchange files of the trade have it |
| Print files written with `WRITE ... AFTER ADVANCING` | The Report Writer: `REPORT SECTION`, `RD` with controls and page limits, report groups with `SOURCE` and `SUM`, `INITIATE`, `GENERATE` and `TERMINATE`, which cobolith does not read yet |
| Binary items as IBM or Micro Focus lay them out | Fujitsu's: `COMP-5` little-endian, `SYNCHRONIZED` as IBM, in a binary control file the survey has to lay out right to read |
| Two-dimensional tables at most | Rate tables by product, sex, entry age and policy year, `OCCURS` three deep, read with subscripts the lineage must follow |
| JCL or POSIX sh | Windows batch files: `set DD_...=`, `call`, `if errorlevel`, `move`, `%1` and `%~dp0`, run by bedrock's own interpreter, as bedrock's runner stands in for JES; sorting by `SORT` statements in the programs, since the platform's sort has no keys |
| Fixed format, or free | Fixed format, columns 8 to 72, with two-byte characters that must not straddle the margin |

## The domain

A life insurer's administration system holds policies for decades and runs by the month:

- **New business:** policies from the sales system, checked for the product's limits on age, term and sum insured, with the insurance age, which rounds at six months, computed from the birth date, and the first premium.
- **Premium collection:** the premiums due each month by payment mode, written as a bank transfer request file to the collection agent, with the payer's name in half-width katakana; the results back a few days later, paid or unpaid with a reason, which start the grace period.
- **Lapse and reinstatement:** a grace period that ends in lapse, and a reinstatement within a year with the arrears paid: a state machine, in force, in grace, lapsed, reinstated, surrendered, matured, paid, the survey should find.
- **Claims:** death and hospitalization claims checked against the policy's state on the event date, waiting periods and exclusions, the benefit from the product table, and the payment file.
- **Surrender:** the cash value from a table by product, entry age and policy year, less any policy loan.
- **Anniversaries:** daily, the policies whose anniversary it is: the age moved on, renewal premiums for renewable term, and maturity benefits.
- **Month-end:** policy reserves by the actuarial tables and the general-ledger summary; the agents' commissions by product and policy year; both printed by the Report Writer.
- **Annual:** the policyholders' notices, and dividends.
- **Maintenance:** changes of address, beneficiary and bank account, and name changes, which touch both the kanji name and the katakana one.

About fifteen programs: loading and unloading the policy master, new business, billing, results, lapse, reinstatement, claims, surrender, anniversaries, reserves, commissions, notices, maintenance, and called modules for the insurance age, the era conversion with the 2019 change inside it, and the cash value lookup. Batch files for opening the system, the daily run, the monthly billing, the monthly close and the annual run; a scheduler starts them, which is in the operations guide, not in the files.

## What was checked first

GnuCOBOL 3.2 compiles Shift_JIS source under bedrock's `-Wall -Werror`: comments, `PIC X` values and `DISPLAY` literals in Japanese come out whole, the characters with a backslash second byte among them, and it accepts a Japanese data name. Its Report Writer works. Its national items do not: `PIC N(4)` holds four of its own code units, each a source byte widened, so a national literal of four characters is truncated and garbled. Gondwana therefore keeps its Japanese text in `PIC X` fields of Shift_JIS bytes, as the code of that age does, and has no `PIC N`; cobolith's national layout stays tested by its own tables alone until a compiler that lays it out is in the toolchain. GnuCOBOL has no Fujitsu configuration; bedrock compiles the dialect with `-std=default`, and the specimen stays within what both compilers accept, as Laurasia stays within Micro Focus and GnuCOBOL.

## Decisions

- **Japanese in the specimen.** The programs, copybooks, batch files and data are Japanese throughout, comments and names included, which is what the dialect is for; the documentation and the Go around it are English, so the showcase stays readable. The repository's rule that all text is English gains this exception, in the same change that adds the specimen. The names are half-width katakana, not kanji: GnuCOBOL takes any byte above 0x7F in a name but reads a two-byte character's second byte as the ASCII symbol it coincides with, so a kanji name compiles or not by its bytes, where single-byte katakana always does, and that is how 1980s Japanese COBOL named its data anyway. File names, report names and program names stay ASCII, since GnuCOBOL makes C identifiers of them. Blueprints are UTF-8: what the survey writes about Shift_JIS source is transcoded. The sources and goldens are Shift_JIS in the working tree and UTF-8 in the repository, by `.gitattributes`, so the compiler reads what NetCOBOL would and the web and the diffs read.
- **Batch files, not a third shell.** The point of the third job control is a reader and a runner that owe nothing to sh: bedrock's `script` gains a batch-file parser into the same model, and a runner that executes the model step by step, since no `cmd.exe` runs on the build machines. The scripts stay the specimen's own, in its own syntax, as the ground rules want.
- **Era dates are a date kind.** The survey tells a date by its name and by what writes it; an era date is three fields that together are a date, and a two-digit year with no era is a date the survey can only mark as one whose century is nowhere in the code.

## Parts

1. **Gondwana runs.** The programs, copybooks and batch files, a hand-written fixture of a few dozen policies across the three eras with one month of days, golden outputs, its README and operations guide. Bedrock compiles the Fujitsu dialect, reads a program's header in its encoding, and parses and runs the batch files. The dependency check and the repository's rules take the third specimen. Nothing in the survey changes yet.

   As built, in #138 and #139: fourteen programs rather than fifteen, named the shop's way, `GLO010` to open, `GLD010` to `GLD090` daily, `GLM010` to `GLM030` monthly, `GLY010` yearly and `GLC010` and `GLC020` called: new business, anniversaries with the renewal of term policies and the maturity of endowments, benefits with death, hospitalization and surrender, maintenance with reinstatement, the collection agent's results, lapse, the unload, billing into the banks' fixed-length file through a `SORT`, reserves and commissions with the Report Writer, dividends, the insurance age and the era conversion. Seventeen copybooks, the era table and the tables' loading among them. Seven batch files, one calling another. The fixture is March 2027, Reiwa 9: 27 policies across Showa, Heisei and Reiwa, the system opened, three business days, the billing, the results and the close, with the agent's results made from the billing's own file. GnuCOBOL decided two things: names are half-width katakana, as above, and `PIC N` is not used. The daily programs append to one journal and one payment file through `SELECT OPTIONAL` and `OPEN EXTEND`, which took blanking the record areas, since a line sequential write refuses a low-value filler. The cash-value and reserve factors reach 1,000 per 1,000 at maturity, so they are four digits before the point.
2. **The survey on it, as it is.** Run it, and write down what breaks and what comes out wrong, program by program, in `gondwana-findings.md`, each finding marked with the part that fixes it. Expected before running: the survey reads source as ASCII, cobolith stops at the `REPORT SECTION`, the Japanese names and literals come out as bytes, amounts in whole yen read as counts, era dates read as text.

   As built: [gondwana-findings.md](gondwana-findings.md). The survey reads none of the programs as they are, for three reasons, a copybook of procedure, the Report Writer and the batch files taken for programs; with the analyzer patched in a throwaway copy, 16 findings, 3 where it stops and 13 where the facts are wrong or missing, each marked with the part that fixes it. The lexer did not break: half-width katakana is one byte a character and the kanji are in comments and literals, so the ASCII reading reads; it is the writing that is not UTF-8. The yen and the era dates came out as expected, as numbers and text; what was not expected was the state machine, drawn from any because the status is tested one paragraph up from where it is set, and the banks' file, whose one write performed from four paragraphs is given every type.
3. **The language.** In cobolith, released and bumped: the Report Writer, as data description and as statements, with what a report group reads for its `SOURCE` and `SUM` items counting as data flow. In Copal: every reader of source takes the dialect's encoding, the blueprints transcode what they quote, the oracle traces and fills records in Shift_JIS, and whatever else part 2 found in the parsing and the layouts. Pangaea's and Laurasia's blueprints stay unchanged where nothing about them changed.

   As built, cobolith 0.8.0 and 0.8.1 and #141: cobolith reads the Report Writer, `REPORT IS` on an FD, the `REPORT SECTION` with its RDs and groups, and `INITIATE`, `GENERATE` and `TERMINATE`, and lays out the lines a report prints as the records of its file, each column where `COLUMN` puts it and named after its line and column when it has no name, so that a report's field origins read as a `WRITE`'s do; NIST's six Report Writer programs read with it, 439 of 459. A source read in Shift_JIS comes out in UTF-8, names, literals and comments alike, transcoded in the lexer, which is cobolith's first dependency; the data the record package reads stays as the file has it. In Copal: the survey and the oracle read source and copybooks in the dialect's encoding, and the batch files' comments through `cobol.ToUTF8`; a `GENERATE` or `TERMINATE` reads each column's `SOURCE` and `SUM` items into the column and writes each line to the file; a copybook whose first token is no level number is a copybook of procedure, its paragraphs the programs' and its banner its description, split from the first paragraph's own comment at the rule lines; and `.bat` is a job, through bedrock's reader. The first golden blueprints of Gondwana: 53 pages, 21 diagrams, 473 rules in 118 tables, all UTF-8. Findings 1, 2, 3, 4 and 11 are fixed; Pangaea's and Laurasia's blueprints did not change.
4. **The facts.** The couplings in the analyses: kinds of yen amounts, era dates and their lineage into Western ones, three-deep subscripts, the Report Writer's output as the program's output in the job network, the batch files as jobs with their datasets and schedule, and the rest of part 2's list. Lineage, the design views and the oracle on Gondwana, with its own golden cases and its batch run through the batch files.

   As built, in #143 to #148: findings 5 to 10 and 12 to 16 are fixed, and 13 needed nothing. Kinds are told by their use and by their names in both languages: a field summed, compared with an amount or named ｷﾝｶﾞｸ is an amount, a rate is one by its suffix alone, a date is one by its name in either language, by being an argument of `INTEGER-OF-DATE`, by being passed to the era conversion, or by its digits being taken apart, and a four-digit year-month with no century is a date. A sort work file needs no DD, and a dataset read whole has the copybook of the record it is moved into. A write performed from several paragraphs takes the record type each path set before the `PERFORM`, and a record type's fields pair with the base record's by name before by order, so a header's name fields are not dates. The state machine reads a paragraph's decisions with what the rules that perform it know, through as many `PERFORM`s as it takes, and a transition's reasons are the conditions the rules part on, so the policy's life is drawn, in force to grace and back, grace to lapse, lapse to in force, covered to matured, surrendered and paid; what a path knows carries on to the paragraphs performed from where it knows it, which took counting a file statement as writing the file's status. Origins are followed through the programs called, so the master's era dates come from the sales system's Western ones, and a move names the bytes a reference modification takes, so a date in the first eight bytes of the command line is those bytes. The oracle runs the batch files through bedrock's runner, with its stand-ins, gives the business date to the bytes a date is taken from, and records Gondwana's cases: 161 of them reach 312 of the 672 rules of its twelve main programs and the programs they call, fewer in proportion than the other two specimens' because the search does not yet make a results file that agrees with its trailer, policies in the states the requests need, or dates in the era a table expects; the six batch files run as a batch, the daily and the close calling the unload, RESULTS ending with 8 for that reason. The hosted run showed two cases and the close coming out differently on Linux, a product not on the product table leaving a SEARCH index past it and the factor read from beyond it being whatever lies there, so bedrock compiles the Fujitsu dialect with GnuCOBOL's runtime checks, the counterpart of NetCOBOL's CHECK option, and such a run ends with return code 1 everywhere, the close's reserve step among them; and GnuCOBOL's trace names a copybook as the file system opened it, PRDLOD.CPY on macOS, so the coverage compares file names without regard to case. The explorer crawls Gondwana's pages, cases included, and a step that runs another script links to it. Finding 7's conversion is seen from the caller's side; what a called program does with each argument in each direction is still read from its own page.
5. **A living Gondwana.** As the bank and the card processor have: a portfolio generated from a seed across the three eras and the products, the days chaining through the month, billing day, results day and the close, a year run to show the anniversaries and the annual run, and a failed day backed out whole. `make gondwana-open` and `make gondwana-days N=31`.

   As built: `gondwana/records` loads the record layouts from the copybooks with cobolith, which took cobolith 0.8.2 reading a copybook in the dialect's encoding, so that a Shift_JIS copybook's fields are found by their katakana names in UTF-8 as a program's are; `gondwana/generate` writes and reads the files by field through them, in Shift_JIS, with a calendar of its own that holds a date in the Western calendar and in its era. The extract is about two thousand policies contracted from Showa 51 to the day before the opening, whole life, term, endowment and medical by weight, monthly and annual payers, each with a birth date giving an entry age within the product, a sum in the units it is sold in, the premium the rate table gives, its policy year, a term policy renewed at each ten years at the attained age or ended when that passed the product's maximum, an endowment past its maturity matured, and a fate drawn for the rest: in force, in grace from a failed debit, lapsed within the year or longer ago, surrendered or paid out on a death; and what its state implies, the paid-to month and the arrears, the reserve the last close left, the dividends the fiscal years' ends credited, now and then a policy loan, and an office closed long ago as the agent of a few old ones. The tables are written by formula, the product table as the fixture has it. For each business day the sales system's new policies come numbered by the day, dated in the Western calendar, one in twenty with something wrong; the branches' requests follow the policies as the last unload left them: deaths by the insured's age, hospitalizations on the medical policies, surrenders of covered and lapsed policies, changes of address, account, name and beneficiary, reinstatements with the arrears paid at the counter, usually in full, and a request a branch should not have sent now and then; and the collection agent answers the request the billing wrote, the banks' names filled in, a debit in sixty or so failed, a few policyholders failing every other month, the trailer agreeing. `gondwana/insurer` runs it through the batch files in the order the operations guide gives: the results on their day before the daily run, the daily run, the billing on the 10th, the close on the month's last business day, the dividends after the close of March; a day that fails is backed out whole, and two insurers from one seed agree byte for byte. `make gondwana-open` and `make gondwana-days N=261` print a line a business day, and `gondwana/run/insurer/data` is the insurer's `GLS_DATA`.

   A fiscal year from one seed, April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027, 261 business days, every step ending with 0 or 4: the master opens with 2,000 policies, 1,601 in force, 33 in grace, 110 lapsed and 256 ended, and closes with 1,870 in force, 29 in grace and 179 lapsed, after 447 new policies, 111 benefits paid, 253 changes applied and 136 records rejected; twelve billings of 18,274 debits in all, 17,839 collected and 435 unpaid; and at the year's end dividends of 18,199,000 yen to 843 policies. Three things the year showed that one month could not. The anniversary step takes only the policies whose contract day is the business date, so an anniversary that falls on a weekend is never taken: 387 of the 1,467 anniversaries the year held were missed, and seven endowments and term policies past their maturity are still covered and still billed; the step should take every anniversary since the control record's last business date, and renew or mature as of the anniversary. The February debit falls on March 1 when the 27th is a Saturday, and its results arrive on the 3rd, after the lapse step of the 1st has lapsed every policy in grace since January's debit, so their February premiums, collected, are rejected; that is the operations guide's schedule, and the year shows what it costs. And the era conversion checks a date against the eras alone, so February 30 is a date to it and the new-business step registers a policy contracted on it; the generator's mistyped date is a year without its century instead, which no era holds.

   The anniversaries, fixed in #150: GLD020 takes every anniversary after the control record's business date, the last the unload moved it to, up to the run's, and the day's own when the two are equal, on the day the system opens and on a rerun; a term policy renews, and an endowment matures, as of the anniversary rather than the run date, so the maturity keeps the contract's day. The same year from the same seed then takes 1,456 of its 1,458 anniversaries and all fourteen maturities, 97 benefits paid with them, and ends with 1,865 in force, 31 in grace and 178 lapsed, dividends of 18,455,000 yen to 851 policies. The late debit and the February 30 stand as the year showed them.
